Making your droid talk with Kyber Echo

Kyber Echo is the sound board inside your droid — the part that holds your sounds and decides when to play them. Your whole library lives on a memory card inside it, and it can play three things at once: the roll of the motors, the dome turning, and the sounds you trigger yourself. The lights and the jaw move along with the sound.

Your radio triggers it, and you set it up from a console the droid serves itself — no app to install, no cable, nothing to dismantle. This guide covers that console page by page, field by field.

01Before you start

You need three things. No tools are required.

  • Your droid, powered up Give it a few seconds. The screen runs through its checks, then settles into its normal display, which means it is ready.
  • Your radio The heart of the whole thing. Once the droid is out and moving, the sticks and switches are what play the sounds — the console only decides which sound each control plays. Everything from section 06 onward assumes you have one.
  • A device with a web browser Anything that joins a Wi-Fi network and opens a web page: a laptop, a desktop, a tablet, a phone. Use the largest screen you have for the first setup — the mapping tables are wide, and you will be typing labels into a lot of rows. Everything is reachable from a small screen too, which is what matters at an event, but the wider tables scroll sideways on it.
Everything in this guide is done from a browser. You never need to take the droid apart, plug anything in, or handle the memory card by hand.

02Opening the console

A new droid has never been given the name and password of your Wi-Fi, so it cannot join it. Instead it creates a small network of its own, and that is the one you connect to the first time.

  1. Look for a network called ECHO_xxxx Join it as you would any other Wi-Fi network. The password is 12345678 unless you have changed it. The four characters at the end identify your droid, so it never clashes with another one nearby.
  2. Open 192.168.4.1 in your browser Type it into the address bar exactly as written. The console loads.
  3. Ignore the "no internet" warning The warning is accurate: this network has no internet connection. It carries the droid's console, which is all it is for.

Once you are connected, the address shown on the droid's screen is always the current one. When in doubt, read it there.

You have 60 seconds. If nobody connects within that first minute, the droid switches its Wi-Fi off and stays quiet: at an event, a live Wi-Fi would compete with your radio for the airwaves. Switch the droid off and on again for a fresh minute.

04Joining your Wi-Fi network

Optional, and worth doing at home. Once the droid has joined your own network, the console is reachable from that same network — no more disconnecting from your Wi-Fi and joining the droid's every time you want it. Out of the house, at an event or anywhere your network is out of range, the droid goes back to making its own — so the console is reachable either way, and there is nothing to switch by hand.

Open the Network page. It is in three parts.

Wi-Fi Status

The current state: which network, which address, how strong the signal. Check here first whenever the console is hard to reach.

Connect to Network

  1. Switch on Try to Connect to WiFi Left off, the droid never looks for your network and always makes its own.
  2. Press Scan, then tap your network in the list Tapping a result fills the name in for you. Typing it by hand is where small, hard-to-spot errors come from.
  3. Enter the password and press Connect Then restart the droid. From then on it joins your network on its own, and falls back to making its own if yours is not there. Its address changes with the network, and 192.168.4.1 no longer applies — always read the current one off the droid's screen before opening the console again.

Create WiFi Network

Changes the name and password of the droid's own network. Leave the name blank to keep the automatic ECHO_xxxx. Setting your own password here is one of the first things to do with a new droid — ahead of the sounds, ahead of the channels, and certainly before the droid goes out in public. It must be at least 8 characters. The console also accepts an empty password, which leaves the network open to anyone within range — there is no good reason to do that on a droid you take out. Either way, a change to the name or the password only takes effect once the droid is restarted: until then the network keeps the name and password it started up with.

Change the password from 12345678. It is the same on every droid that leaves the shop, and it is printed in this guide, so anyone nearby at an event can join your droid's network and take over the console. Set a password of your own here, before the first event rather than after.
The droid's own network does not stay up on its own. Go a minute with nobody connected to the console and the droid switches its Wi-Fi off entirely, and leaves it off until it is restarted. That is a second line of defence behind the password: a network nobody is using is one nobody can find either. The minute starts again each time someone is connected, so it will not cut you off mid-session.
If the droid loses your network it does not keep trying to reconnect: repeated attempts would keep it transmitting, in the band your radio needs. Switch it off and on again when you next want the console.

05Adding your own sounds

Do this before anything else on the Sounds page: you cannot point a button at a sound that is not there yet. Both MP3 and WAV files are accepted.

Start from an empty card. If sounds are already on it — from testing, or from whoever set the droid up before you — they will get in your way later. Your buttons point at numbers, not at names, so a leftover file sitting on a number you meant to use is the one that plays. Clear the card before you add anything: on the SD Card page, tick the box in the table header to select every file, then delete. If you have the card in your hand and a reader, formatting it does the same job and leaves nothing behind at all.

Go to SD Card. Drag your files onto the upload area, or tap it to browse. Several at once is fine. MP3 files are converted as they are uploaded, so you can add whatever you have.

Names are how sounds are identified

Every sound is a number from 001 to 999, and that number is what you will select later on the Sounds page. Add a name after the number if you like: the droid ignores it, and it makes the list much easier to read.

Three digits, no more and no fewer. Without a number the file is ignored outright; with four, the droid reads the first three and files it under a number you cannot reach.

Listening before you use a file

Each file offers two ways to hear it, and the difference matters:

ButtonWhat you hear
Play on deviceThe droid's own speaker. This is the real test: it is exactly what your audience will hear.
PreviewYour own speakers, through the browser. Useful for picking a file quickly; it tells you nothing about how it will sound on the droid.
RenameChanges the number a sound answers to. See the warning below.

Renaming can silently rewire a button. Your buttons point at numbers, not at names. Rename 012_Beep.wav to 031_Beep.wav and every button that played 012 now plays whatever else is at 012, or nothing at all. Decide on your numbering early and keep it.
Switch your radio transmitter off before uploading. While the droid is hearing the radio, this page greys itself out: uploading, renaming and deleting all stop working, because file transfers and the radio link cannot share the airwaves. Switch the transmitter off and the page becomes available again on its own, with no reload. Preview keeps working throughout, as it never involves the droid.

If a sound does not play

Check the name before anything else. The droid reads the first three characters and nothing more, and all three must be digits. 001.wav is right; 0001.wav is not — that fourth digit makes the droid read it as number 000, which is a number no button can be set to. The file sits there in the list, perfectly good, and nothing will ever play it.

The sound itself is rarely the problem. Whatever you upload through the console is re-encoded on the way in — MP3 or WAV, mono or stereo, quiet or loud, it arrives in the one format the droid plays. A file that was copied onto the card with a reader instead has had none of that done to it, and that is where format trouble comes from.

06Channels — assigning your radio's controls

Your radio sends the droid a set of numbered channels. The droid cannot tell which is which until you say so, and this is the page where you do it.

This is the page that makes the droid playable: everything after it depends on getting these drop-downs right. It takes about a minute.

Set thisTo the channel that carries…
Speed ChannelYour drive stick. The rolling sound rises and falls with it.
Dome MovementYour dome control, with its own sound doing the same.
Effect 1 / 2 / 3 ButtonA switch or button you want to trigger sounds with. Each one gives you two triggers; see Sounds.
Effects / Rolling / Dome VolumeA knob or slider, one per family of sounds. Assign at least one: the console has no volume control of its own, so with all three left on None nothing can turn the droid down. The same channel can be given to all three, and a single knob then sets everything.
KyberpadKyberpad, if you use it. Its own card sits at the foot of this page, and it needs a second channel for the page switch.

How to set them

Do not work this out on paper. Every card on this page shows its channel moving in real time, so the reliable method is to pick a channel, move the control, and watch.

  1. Pick a channel from the drop-down
  2. Move that stick or flick that switch
  3. Watch the barIf the bar moves, the channel is correct. If it does not, try the next one. All of them together take under a minute.

If no channel makes the bar move, stop working through them and look at the top right of the page instead. The badge there tells you whether anything is arriving from the radio at all, and while it reads red no drop-down will help.

Worked example

Your drive stick is on channel 1, and you have a two-way switch on channel 4 you want to trigger sounds with.

CH1
Effect 1 Button
CH4
Everything else
None

Push the drive stick and the Speed bar sweeps. Flick the switch and the Effect 1 bar jumps to one side, then the other. That is all this page needs.

Leave anything you do not have set to None. Those cards then disappear from the Sounds page, so that page only ever shows what your droid can actually do.

A volume knob works on sounds already playing. Turn it down halfway through a line and you hear that line fade, not the next one.

Kyberpad — connecting it to the droid

Optional, and worth setting up if Kyberpad is running on your radio: it takes you from six triggers to forty-five. If it is not, skip ahead — the three effect switches work very well on their own, and everything else in this guide applies just the same.

Kyberpad is a keypad that runs on the radio itself: fifteen keys on each of three pages, tapped on the radio's screen. The droid does not know it exists; it only sees the channel that comes out of it. The radio therefore has to be set up first, and the droid second. Deciding what each key plays comes afterwards, on the Sounds page.

On the radio. Two things to send out, each on its own channel:

  1. Send the Kyberpad output to a channel Full travel, no trim, no expo, and no slew.
  2. Send the page switch to a second channel Same settings. Without it you only ever reach page 1.
Slew is the most common cause of misfires. It slides the channel gradually from one value to the next, so a single key press passes through every key on the way and the droid plays the whole page instead of the sound you asked for. Turn it off.

In the console. The Kyberpad card, at the foot of this page, needs the two channels you just set up and the number of pages you use. Everything below that line is a live readout, there to be watched rather than filled in.

Press a key on the radio and watch the badge. It should name the key you actually pressed, on the page you are actually on. If it does, the setup is correct and everything that follows is a matter of choosing sounds. If it does not, nothing further will work: resolve it before moving on.

One mistake the console catches for you is a channel used twice. If Kyberpad shares a channel with a switch, a knob, or its own page switch, an amber line appears in the card naming both, and you can fix it on the spot.

A key already held when the droid starts listening does nothing. The droid waits until it has seen Kyberpad at rest before it accepts anything, so switching the radio on with a key pressed cannot fire a sound by itself. Let go once and it responds normally. The same applies every time the signal comes back after being lost.

There is an Advanced section holding the values the droid expects from each key. It already matches Kyberpad as delivered. Leave it alone unless you have deliberately changed the key values on the radio. If the values no longer match, Restore Kyberpad defaults returns them to the delivered settings.

07Sounds — deciding what plays when

One page answers a single question, in tables. The tabs across the top are your three Kyberpad pages, plus Effects for everything driven directly from the radio.

Kyberpad — choosing what each key plays

How many page tabs you see is decided back on Channels. With no Kyberpad channel assigned there are none at all, and the page tells you so instead of showing an empty table. With a Kyberpad channel but no page switch, you get Page 1 on its own — which is also why the Pages box on Channels stays greyed out until you give it a page switch. Set both, and you get one tab per page you chose there: one, two or three.

Fifteen rows, one per key, in the same order as the keys on your radio screen. Three things to fill in:

ColumnWhat to put in it
LabelA name for your own use — the droid ignores it completely. Give the key the same name here that it has on the radio, so the two screens read alike when you come back to them months later.
Sound Min / Sound MaxThe range of sounds this key draws from. The same number in both boxes means one fixed sound, every time.
RandomOff, the key steps through its range in order. On, it shuffles, and plays every sound in the range before repeating any of them.

Next to the tabs there is a Stop Button box: the number of the key, 1 to 15, that silences all playback instead of playing a sound. Each page has its own, and 0 means that page has no stop key. The row you choose is marked STOP in the table.

There is nothing to press when you are done. The page saves a second or so after you stop typing, and confirms briefly. Switching tabs saves anything still pending first, so you cannot lose a page by leaving it.

The Effects tab

Everything the radio drives directly lives here, Kyberpad or not: the driving and dome sounds, and the three effect switches. Kyberpad adds to these rather than replacing them, so this tab is worth filling in either way — and if you do not use Kyberpad, it is your entire setup. Cards appear here only for the controls you assigned back on Channels.

CardWhat you set
Rolling SoundThe sound that runs while driving, and how it behaves. The Min values apply at a standstill, the Max values at full speed.
Dome SoundThe same idea, driven by the dome instead.
Sound Effect 1 / 2 / 3Two rows each: Button 1 for pushing the switch one way, Button 2 for the other. Same three columns as a Kyberpad page.

Rolling Sound and Dome Sound are the two cards with sliders. Each picks a single file and loops it for as long as its control asks, stretching it between the values you set. The four sliders are the same on both.

SliderWhat it does
Volume MinHow loud the sound is the instant the control leaves centre. Set it low and the droid creeps in quietly; set it high and it announces itself the moment you touch the stick.
Volume MaxHow loud at full travel. Everything between the two is spread evenly across the stick, so the gap between them is the effect: a wide gap sounds like effort building, and the same value in both gives a sound that never changes.
Pitch Min / MaxThe same idea for pitch. 1.0 is the file exactly as recorded; below it the sound drags and deepens, above it tightens and rises. The range runs from 0.50 to 1.50 — half speed to half again as fast.

Play sends the chosen file to the droid so you can hear it without touching the radio. The button turns into Stop while it runs, and pressing it again silences it.

Two ways to set the same card

Narrow spans read as a real machine; wide ones read as a cartoon. Both are valid — it depends on the droid you want.

Restrained
Volume 30 → 80 %, Pitch 0.90 → 1.10
Expressive
Volume 10 → 100 %, Pitch 0.60 → 1.30
The rolling sound follows where the stick is. The dome sound follows whether the stick is moving. Push the drive stick half way and hold it there and the rolling sound keeps running, at the volume and pitch half way earns. Do the same on the dome and the sound starts, then fades out about a third of a second later, because the droid has stopped seeing the stick move. Keep turning and it keeps going. This catches people out, so it is worth trying both once before you decide the dome file is faulty.

Two smaller differences follow from that. The dome reaches its loudest at about half travel, where the drive stick needs all of it — it was made deliberately eager, since dome moves are short. And both ignore direction: forwards and backwards, clockwise and anticlockwise all sound the same. Neither cuts off abruptly; both fade.

The effect cards work nothing like those two. That two-row split is how three switches give you six different triggers. Resting in the middle does nothing, which is what makes a spring-loaded switch a good fit.

Some radios go further: a three-position toggle can be given over to two push buttons fitted into the radio's own case, one for each row of the card. The droid sees no difference — it is still one channel with three states, with the middle one meaning nothing pressed — but a button that springs back under your thumb is much closer to how you actually want to fire a sound than a lever you have to flick and remember to return.

08Jaw servo (experimental)

If your droid has a moving jaw, this makes it open and close in time with the voice. It follows the effects and Kyberpad only: driving and dome sounds leave the jaw closed, which is the intended behaviour.

SettingHow to set it
Min — closedWhere the jaw sits at rest. Nudge it until the jaw is just closed, and no further.
Max — openHow far it opens on a loud sound. Raise it until the movement is visible across a room, then reduce it slightly.
ThresholdHow loud a sound has to be to count as speech. Too low and the jaw moves when nothing is being said; too high and quiet lines are missed.
ReleaseHow fast it closes again. Higher is smoother and more natural; lower closes abruptly.
Enable / ReverseOn or off, and which way it swings. This depends on how your jaw is built.

Every change is saved as you drag, so the practical method is to work with a sound playing on a loop: adjust, listen, look, adjust again.

Set the two angles before switching it on. Start with a small gap between them and open it up gradually. A jaw driven further than it can physically travel will buzz, overheat, and eventually break.

Set the Threshold with a real line of dialogue, never a test tone: a steady tone is the one sound that never behaves like speech.

10Updating

From time to time a new version of the droid's software is published. The Dashboard tells you and installs it for you.

When there is something new, a badge appears at the top of the console, on every page. Open the Dashboard: the Online Updates card names the new version, its date, its size, and lists what changed, so you can decide before you commit to anything. Your own version is on the same page, under Firmware.

What happens when you press install

Press Download and install and the console works through four steps, naming each one as it goes. Nothing is asked of you in between.

  1. Downloading firmware… Your browser fetches the new version from the internet — not the droid, which has no connection of its own. The percentage is the download.
  2. Uploading… The same file goes from your browser to the droid over your local connection. The percentage restarts from zero; this is the slower half.
  3. Flashing firmware… Shown in amber, because this is the part not to interrupt. The droid is writing the new version into itself.
  4. Rebooting in 5s… It counts down, the droid restarts, and the page reloads itself. If it does not come back, reconnect to the droid as you did the first time — the update itself is already done.

Everything you set up survives it: your sounds, your buttons, your labels, your Wi-Fi. An update replaces the droid's software, not its settings.

An interrupted update leaves the droid on the version it already had. The new one is only switched to once it has arrived complete and intact — so a dropped Wi-Fi connection, a closed tab or a failed download costs you the wait and nothing else. You will see a red Download failed or Upload failed, and you can simply try again. That said, do not cut the power during Flashing firmware…; it is the one moment worth staying still for.

What the card can say

It saysIt means
Up to dateNothing to do.
Update availableA newer version is available. The install button is on the same card.
OfflineYour browser could not reach the internet within about six seconds. Normal when you are on the droid's own network with nothing else to hand, and not a fault. Check again retries.
Not publishedThe site answered, but has nothing to offer yet. Different from Offline, and deliberately so: one means nothing to install, the other means you could not ask.
Ahead of releaseYou are running a version newer than the published one. Unusual, and nothing to fix.
The checking is done by your browser, never by the droid. That sounds like a detail and is the reason the whole thing works away from home: with the droid making its own network and your device holding a mobile connection at the same time, the console can still find and fetch an update the droid could never reach by itself. It also explains Offline — it describes your device's connection, not the droid's health.
Switch your radio transmitter off first. The console will not let you start while the droid is hearing the radio: an update is far too large to survive the interference. Turn the transmitter off and the button becomes available.

Installing a file by hand

Below the automatic card there is a drop area for a .bin file, for the day someone sends you one directly rather than publishing it. Drop it in or tap to browse; anything that is not a .bin is refused on the spot, with a red mark against the name. Then press upload, and it picks up at step two of the list above — the download has already happened, by whatever means the file reached you.

11Backup and reset

Four buttons at the foot of the Dashboard, in rising order of consequence.

ButtonWhat happens
Save ConfigDownloads everything you have set up as a single file: channels, every button, every label, the jaw. It lands in your downloads folder as echo-config- followed by the date and time.
Restore ConfigLoads one back in, then restarts the droid. Also how you copy a finished setup onto a second droid.
Reboot DeviceA clean restart. Harmless, and occasionally enough to clear a problem.
Factory ResetClears every setting and starts over — including the way you reach the droid. Your sounds are untouched: the library stays, the buttons pointing at it do not.
Press Save Config once the droid is set up the way you want it. Labelling 45 Kyberpad keys takes a long time; restoring them takes one action.

The backup file

Its name does not matter; its contents do. Rename it, file it away, move it to another computer — it still restores. What the console checks is that the file really is an Echo configuration, and it refuses anything else outright rather than half-applying it. A file with the right name but the wrong contents is refused just the same.

A backup taken from an older version of the droid's software is still usable. It restores what it contains and leaves everything else as it was, so nothing is lost simply because the file predates a setting.

No password ever goes into the file. Neither the one for the network you asked the droid to join, nor the one guarding the droid's own network. They are left out deliberately, because restoring never puts them back anyway — so carrying them would have given away the password to your home network every time you sent the file to someone. You can open the file in any text editor and check.

Restoring leaves your Wi-Fi entirely alone for the same reason: the network settings in a file are read past, never applied. Someone else's backup therefore brings across their channels, their ranges and their forty-five labels without ever taking the console away from you.

The one thing that follows from this: after restoring onto a droid that has never been on your network, you still have to give it your Wi-Fi by hand, on the Network page. Two fields, against the hours the rest of the file saves you.

What Factory Reset costs you

It does what it says, and that includes the settings you reach the droid through.

A factory reset makes the droid forget your Wi-Fi, and puts its own password back to the default. If you were reaching the console through your home network, that route is gone the moment it restarts. Expect to lose the page, and expect the password you chose for the droid's own network to be back to 12345678.

Getting back in is the same as the very first time, and takes a minute:

  1. Join the network called ECHO_xxxx The password is 12345678 again. Any name you had given that network is gone too, so look for ECHO_ followed by four characters.
  2. Open 192.168.4.1 The console loads, empty of your settings but with every sound still on the card.
  3. Restore your backup, or start again This is the moment the file from Save Config earns its keep. Without one, everything from section 04 onwards has to be done by hand.

Change the droid's network password again straight afterwards. A reset quietly undoes that, and it is easy to forget you are back on the factory one.

12Wiring

For whoever builds or repairs the droid rather than uses one. Everything before this section assumes the board is already wired; this is the section that says how.

This section is a draft. The pin numbers below are correct — they are the ones the firmware is compiled with — but the connector types, wire colours, power supply and mounting are still to be written.

The board

A Waveshare ESP32-S3-LCD-1.47B: a small board carrying the screen, a microSD slot and an RGB LED already, so those three need no wiring of their own. Everything in the table below is something you connect to it.

What connects where

Pin numbers are GPIO numbers, the ones printed on the board.

Radio receiverIts SBUS output to 2. One wire: the droid only ever listens, it never answers the receiver.

13Troubleshooting

In rough order of how often each one happens.

No sound at all

Go to the SD Card page and press Play on device on any file. If that is silent too, the problem is the sound library, not your button mapping. If it plays, the sound is fine and the button pointing at it is not: check on the Sounds page that the numbers match the files you actually have. A file you can see in the list but cannot select anywhere is almost always misnamed — three digits, no more and no fewer.

Everything plays at about half volume

The radio is off, and you have given one of the volume knobs a channel. With no signal to read, the droid settles at half rather than guessing — a droid started with the radio off should not shout. Switch the transmitter on and the knob takes over immediately.

I cannot turn the droid down

There is no volume control in the console: the only way to change the volume is a knob or slider on the radio. If all three volume channels are on None on the Channels page, nothing can turn it down. Assign at least one — the same channel can go in all three.

The console will not open

On the droid's own network, the Wi-Fi has almost certainly switched itself off: it does that after a minute with nobody connected, and stays off until the next restart. Switch the droid off and on, then connect without dawdling. On your home network, its address may have changed — read the current one off the droid's screen. It does not rejoin your network by itself after losing it.

The radio does nothing

Open the Channels page and look at the top right before anything else. A red Disconnected badge means nothing at all is arriving: check the transmitter is on and bound, and no drop-down will help until it turns green. If it is green, move a stick and watch the bars: something moving on the wrong card means your channel numbers need swapping.

The dome sound stops while I hold the stick

That is deliberate, and it is the one place the dome behaves differently from the driving sound. Rolling follows where the stick is, so holding it keeps the sound going; the dome follows whether the stick is moving, and fades out about a third of a second after it stops. Keep turning and the sound continues. Nothing is wrong with the file.

A card or a tab is missing from the Sounds page

The Sounds page only ever shows what your droid can actually do. A missing Dome Sound or Sound Effect 2 card means that control is still on None back on Channels. Missing page tabs work the same way: no Kyberpad channel and there are none at all, a Kyberpad channel without a page switch and you get Page 1 alone.

A Kyberpad key plays the wrong sound — or plays several

Several in a row means slew is still switched on in your radio's mixer. Turn it off. Check the Kyberpad card too: if an amber line names two things sharing one channel, they are fighting over it and roughly half your presses will go astray. Consistently the wrong key means the values on the radio no longer match what the droid expects: press Restore Kyberpad defaults unless you changed them deliberately. And a key held down at the moment the radio comes on, or at the moment the signal returns, is ignored until you let go once.

Everything is greyed out and nothing will upload

Your radio transmitter is on. Uploading files and installing updates both stop while the droid is hearing the radio, because the two cannot share the airwaves. An amber banner at the top of the page says so. Switch the transmitter off and the page becomes available again within a second or two, with no reload.

An update stopped part way

Nothing is broken. The droid only switches to a new version once it has arrived complete, so an interrupted update leaves it exactly as it was, running the version it had. You will have seen a red Download failed or Upload failed; press the button again. If the download is what keeps failing, it is your device's internet connection rather than the droid — the droid never downloads anything itself.

I reset the droid and now I cannot reach it

A factory reset clears the Wi-Fi along with everything else. The droid has forgotten your network, its own network is called ECHO_xxxx again, and its password is back to 12345678. Join that network, open 192.168.4.1, and restore your backup. Then change that password again straight away.

A change was not saved

Saving is automatic but not instant: it waits a second or so after your last change, then confirms with a brief message. Closing the page or cutting the power within that gap loses the change. If no confirmation appeared at all, the console had already lost contact with the droid.

None of the above

The Logs page keeps the droid's most recent messages. Open it, make the problem happen again, and read what appears. Most failures are reported there in plain words.
